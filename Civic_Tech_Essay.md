
Introduction
Civic technology refers to digital tools and resources that promote engagement and transparency between governments, institutions, and the public. These tools can exist in a variety of ways, such as data portals with important research results, neighborhood incident reporting applications, or even city weather warnings. Thus, civic technology is essential to supporting a Smart City, particularly for the mutual benefits it produces for residents and city officials.  

Breaking Down Barriers
As technology evolves, city officials and local governments have made efforts to capitalize on accessibility opportunities. The use of the internet to access critical resources reduces administrative burdens like having to go to a physical office to apply for social services, which typically requires someone to fill out paperwork, gather supplemental documents, take time off work, and travel to offices not typically located in close proximity. With civic technology, this process simply requires an individual to have a phone or a computer connected to WIFI to access a website. This form of civic technology promotes public health and the use of social services amongst residents who tend to be vulnerable to disparities. 

Promoting Civilian Agency 
Civic technology also has the potential to improve the trust civilians have in government agencies by improving communication between both entities. Reporting tools that communities can use to inform their local leaders of flooding, trash, noise or failing infrastructure, gives residents a voice and chance to communicate with their corner. Simultaneously, When residents report issues from their local communities, government agencies can work to tackle problems before they escalate or wait to identify concerns by surveying communities themselves. Typically, such data is then collected and published publicly. When it takes this form, it becomes useful for researchers and organizers who can use it to produce reports or understand gaps in service. 

Ultimately Civic technology can serve various purposes but it ultimately contributes to an improved relationship between residents and their city. Moving forward it is important that Governments invest in the maintenance and accessibility of civic technology to expand its reach and benefits.
